# Commercial offerings surround the reference architecture

Reviewed September 29, 2026.

NVIDIA's DOCA page explicitly lists NVIDIA AI Enterprise as the production-support route for BlueField deployments. This is evidence of a commercial software-support offering around a platform component, not evidence that OpenShell or Sentry automatically receive the same support entitlement.

Source: [DOCA product page, Deploy in Production](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/networking/products/software/doca/).

Dell describes incorporating OpenShell and the Sentry reference design into Dell AI Factory with NVIDIA. Its September 28 article directs customers to their account team for configuration-specific deployment guidance. Some server configurations are described as being engineered, so the announcement is not an inventory or delivery-date guarantee.

Source: [Dell's platform integration announcement](https://www.dell.com/en-us/blog/a-new-era-of-ai-agents-demands-a-new-security-model/).

BlueField itself has a hardware product portfolio and purchasing channels. Hardware acquisition, software entitlements, deployment services, and support should therefore be treated as distinct procurement questions.

Source: [BlueField product page](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/networking/products/data-processing-unit/).

The reviewed platform pages do not establish a unified subscription price, service-level agreement, or self-service managed fleet service. The OpenShell Apache-2.0 license must not be assumed to cover Sentry or the complete DOCA stack; DOCA documents its own EULA and component licenses. A separate Sentry license and independently reproducible installation path remain unverified in this review, not proven absent.

Sources: [platform overview](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/solutions/ai/agent-safety/), [DOCA documentation and licensing index](https://docs.nvidia.com/doca/sdk/index.html).
